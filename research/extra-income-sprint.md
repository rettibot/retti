## 2. The plays

### Play 1: Cash out what you already own (this week)

Do this first, because it's the fastest money in the plan.

1. List every plugin license you haven't opened in 6 months, plus spare hardware: interfaces,
   controllers, mics, stands, cables in bulk, and the T5Vs if you no longer need them for paid work.
2. Check each developer's transfer rules (fee, one-time only, registration locks).
3. Post on the KVR **Sell & Buy** forum (bump every 48 h) and on KnobCloud. Put hardware on Reverb, or sell
   locally to avoid about 8% in fees plus shipping.

**Why now:** Black Friday brings the year's biggest discounts on *new* licenses and floods the used
market. Sell before about Nov 10.

**Rules:**
- Take payment before transferring (PayPal Goods & Services).
- Transfer only through the developer's official process.
- Never hand over a raw serial you've already registered.

**Template (KVR post):**
> **[WTS] {Plugin} {version}, ${price}.** Official transfer via {developer process}; {I/buyer} pay the
> {fee/no fee}. PayPal G&S. KVR feedback: {link}. Open to bundles.

### Play 7: Gear flipping

Flipping works when three things hold: the item sells fast, you know its true value, and you can resell
within weeks. Used audio gear passes all three *for you*. Domains failed all three. Plugin licenses fail
on timing, because Black Friday drops prices, and on transfer friction.

**Rules:**
- **Only popular models** with lots of recent Reverb sales: SM7B-class mics, Neumann TLM 102/103, UA
  Volt/Apollo Twin, Focusrite, Yamaha HS, ADAM T-series, popular controllers and synths.
- **Buy at ≤ 65–70% of the recent median sold price** from local classifieds, where sellers just want it
  gone. Test it before paying.
- **Relist within 48 h** with good photos. Reverb takes about 5% plus 3.19% + $0.49, more for
  cross-border or currency conversion. Local resale avoids that.
- **Bankroll $300–$600, at most $300 per item.** If it hasn't sold in 14 days, cut the price to the
  median.
- **Example math:** 2–4 flips at $60–$150 profit is **$120–$600**.

**Plugin licenses, if you want to try:** wait until after Cyber Monday. Buy current versions only, and
only from developers with free, repeatable, self-service transfers. Spend at most $100 at a time, and
ideally find a buyer (a "WTB" post) *before* you buy.

## 3. Buying the MM-520 as a non-EU resident in Stockholm

The best route is **Thomann, delivered to you in Stockholm, with Thomann refunding the VAT itself**.
Thomann's FAQ describes how it works:

1. Order with your **home (non-EU) address as the billing address**. It must match your passport or ID.
   Use a Stockholm address you'll be at for delivery.
2. Test it there. Thomann has a 30-day money-back guarantee. Returns must be complete, in original
   packaging and without signs of use, and items that touch the body are excluded. Check before ordering
   whether headphones count.
3. When you leave, have **Swedish customs stamp the paper invoice** at Arlanda. Bring the headphones
   along, still in their box. The export must happen within 3 months of purchase.
4. **Post the original stamped invoice** to Thomann (a PDF isn't accepted). They refund the VAT to your
   card. That's the whole VAT, not the 12–19% that Global Blue-type services return.
5. Pay whatever import tax your home country charges above its allowance.

Before ordering, email `international@thomann.de` to confirm they'll refund *Swedish* VAT on a delivery
to Sweden. Also compare their ex-VAT price with what you'd pay at home. Audeze often costs more in the
EU: the only EU listing I found was €2,191, sold out. Also check Thomann's B-Stock page; they've carried
MM-500 B-Stock before.
